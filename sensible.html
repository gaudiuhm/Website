The Problem

Humans experience sensory overload when sound, light, and environmental stimuli exceed what the brain can comfortably process. But there is currently no way to quantify this experience or detect it before it becomes overwhelming.

01

Sensory overload is common. Millions experience overwhelm from noise, light, crowds, and constant stimulation.

02

Environments are becoming more intense. Offices, schools, and public facilities expose us to continuous sensory input.

03

Overload is difficult to detect early. People only realize they're overwhelmed once stress or anxiety has already begun.

04

There is no way to measure sensory load. Unlike heart rate or sleep, sensory stimulation has no clear metric.

The Opportunity

What if we could measure sensory environments the same way we measure heart rate?

A new measurable signal: Sensory Overload Score

A simple metric that quantifies the sensory load of an environment and alerts users before they reach their threshold.

The Solution

A real-time sensory scoring system that helps users anticipate overload.

The system monitors environmental stimuli and physiological signals continuously, surfacing a simple metric before the user reaches their threshold.

  1. Light
  2. Sound
  3. Crowd Density
  4. Heart Rate
Use Cases

Who benefits from Sensible?

🎓

Student in a Lecture Hall

Overlapping conversations, bright lighting, and movement quickly raise sensory stimulation. Sensible detects the rising load and alerts the student before it becomes overwhelming, recommending a quieter seat or Calm Mode.

🚇

Commuter on the Subway

Noise spikes, crowd density, and motion contribute to sensory stress. Sensible monitors these inputs and predicts overload — suggesting stepping off for a break or enabling noise reduction.

💼

Office Worker

Fluctuating stimulation from conversations, screens, and movement throughout the day. Sensible identifies patterns and highlights peak stress periods so the employee can schedule breaks proactively.

The Future

Sensory tracking as the next frontier of wellness.

As technology continues to integrate with daily life, tools that understand and support human sensory experiences will become increasingly important. Future systems may detect, predict, and adapt to environments in real time.

Environments that adapt to people — smart buildings, classrooms, and workplaces automatically adjusting stimuli.

Personal sensory awareness — wearable devices that help individuals understand how their bodies react to different environments.

A new dimension of wellness — sensory tracking as essential for mental and emotional balance as fitness tracking is for physical health.
